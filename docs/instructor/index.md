## About the program

This course was built around a specific observation: most self-study security material teaches tools,
and very little of it teaches **judgement** — when a technique is appropriate, what the evidence
actually supports, and how to explain a finding to someone who will act on it.

The curriculum therefore runs from foundations to capstone with three commitments:

1. **Defender-first.** Offensive technique is taught so that defence can be improved, and every
   technique is paired with how it is detected or prevented.
2. **Practical, but safe.** All work happens in isolated labs, against intentionally vulnerable
   applications, with explicit authorization boundaries.
3. **Written evidence.** Every module produces an artefact a professional would recognise: a report, a
   detection, a hardened host, an assessment.

The full rationale, module catalogue, and hours are in the [roadmap](../curriculum/roadmap.md).

## A note on the material's status

Levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 are published, Level 3 has six of its eight modules, and assessment is published across all
levels: 200 keyed questions, five integrated assessments and six capstone briefs. One Level 5 module (CYB-507,
professional reporting) and two Level 3 modules remain planned. Every page states its own status, and planned
content is labelled rather than presented as available.
